    Tepic, Nayarit

    This is who we are, too.

    We come from a land of mountains, mangroves and sea, also home to the Wixárika people. This is our tribute, in pixel art.

    Something is on the loose around here.

    There are 20 hiding on this page. Can you find them?

    20 of 20 found

    You found them all!

    Who could it be?

    Jaguar

    Sierra de Vallejo

    Panthera onca

    Nayarit's top predator: it ranges from the Sierra de Vallejo in the south to the Marismas Nacionales in the north.

    Who could it be?

    American crocodile

    San Blas

    Crocodylus acutus

    It lives in the wetlands of San Blas, where the Kiekari crocodile sanctuary lets you see it up close.

    Who could it be?

    Olive ridley sea turtle

    Nayarit coast

    Lepidochelys olivacea

    Listed as endangered under Mexican regulations. It can be seen off the Nayarit coast; the Marietas Islands matter to it, though it does not nest there.

    Who could it be?

    Blue-footed booby

    Isla Isabel

    Sula nebouxii

    Many think it lives only in the Galápagos, but it is also found in Nayarit: on Isla Isabel and in San Blas.

    Who could it be?

    Humpback whale

    Banderas Bay

    Megaptera novaeangliae

    The Marietas Islands, in Banderas Bay, are key to its reproduction. It can also be spotted from San Blas.

    Who could it be?

    Green kingfisher

    Riviera Nayarit

    Chloroceryle americana

    A small bird that can be seen year-round on the Riviera Nayarit.

    Who could it be?

    Centzontle

    Four hundred voices

    Mimus polyglottos

    Its name comes from Nahuatl and means “four hundred voices”, because it imitates the songs of other birds. It gives our Centzontle point of sale its name.

    Who could it be?

    Magnificent frigatebird

    Isla Isabel

    Fregata magnificens

    It nests on Isla Isabel, home to large seabird colonies. It stands out for its showy courtship.

    Who could it be?

    Bottlenose dolphin

    Banderas Bay

    Tursiops truncatus

    Also known locally as tonina. In Banderas Bay it is usually seen near shore, between 500 and 2,000 meters from land.

    Who could it be?

    Coati

    Also known as tejón

    Nasua narica

    It is omnivorous: it eats fruit, insects and lizards, and digs through the soil with its snout to find food. It usually travels in groups.

    Who could it be?

    Black iguana

    Endemic to Mexico

    Ctenosaura pectinata

    It lives along the Pacific coast, including Nayarit, in low forests. It is threatened by hunting and habitat loss.

    Who could it be?

    White-tailed deer

    A guide in Wixárika culture

    Odocoileus virginianus

    It lives across most of Mexico and has been recorded by camera traps in Nayarit. For the Wixárika people it is a central figure, a guide and teacher.

    Who could it be?

    Mangrove

    Marismas Nacionales

    Mangrove forest

    It forms the mangrove forests of the Marismas Nacionales, the biosphere reserve in northern Nayarit.

    Who could it be?

    Agave

    Native to Mexico

    Agave

    A plant with thick, pointed leaves, native to Mexico, home to a great variety of species.

    Who could it be?

    Prickly pear (nopal)

    Native to Mexico

    Opuntia

    A cactus native to Mexico, found on the national coat of arms and in kitchens across the country.

    Who could it be?

    Ponytail palm (elephant's foot)

    Native to Mexico

    Beaucarnea recurvata

    A Mexican plant of tropical forests, with a trunk that widens sharply at the base. It is listed as threatened in Mexico and cannot be taken from the wild without a permit.

    Who could it be?

    Breadnut (capomo)

    Recorded in Nayarit

    Brosimum alicastrum

    A tree also known as ramón. Its seed, known as the Maya nut, feeds both people and animals.

    Who could it be?

    Hikuri (peyote)

    Sacred to the Wixárika people

    Lophophora williamsii

    A cactus sacred to the Wixárika people, central to their pilgrimages to Wirikuta and their ceremonies. We show it here for its cultural value.

    Who could it be?

    God's eye (tsikuri)

    Wixárika art

    Wixárika art

    A woven piece of colored diamonds linked to the cardinal directions and to protection. It is given to newborns, and a diamond of a new color is added each year.

    Who could it be?

    Beaded gourd bowl

    Wixárika art

    Wixárika art

    Gourd bowls decorated with beads are part of Wixárika art and are used to gather hikuri.

    Our own illustrations, respectfully inspired by Wixárika culture. They are not works by Wixárika artists.
